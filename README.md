# Everyday Things, Two Lines, Longer Run — entry

## About this entry

The everyday thing here is a washing machine, explained to a 10-year-old as
a merry-go-round that gives your clothes a bath while they ride it. I picked
that pairing because both halves of it are things a kid has physically done:
sat on a spinning ride and sat in a tub of soapy water. Put together, the
picture explains the machine's whole visible behaviour in one go — why the
drum turns, why there is water sloshing inside, why the clothes come out
cleaner than they went in — without a single technical word. The second line
then does the honest accounting the brief demands. It names the mapping
plainly: the ride stands for the spinning drum, the bath stands for the wash
water moving over the clothes. And it admits the two places where the
picture quietly lies: the soap, not the spinning, does the real scrubbing,
and the final hard spin that wrings the clothes nearly dry has no part in
the fairground image at all. I tried a toaster version first — a tiny
tanning bed for bread — but that analogy leaves a kid thinking heat is the
whole story, and its second line had to spend itself correcting the first.
This one holds up: vivid on line one, truthful on line two, 344 characters
in total, one sentence per line, exactly as the contest asks.

## Running it

`python3 main.py` writes the entry to `/output/entry.txt` (override the
directory with `ENTRY_OUTPUT_DIR`) and refuses to write anything that
breaks the gate.
